Read the linked article and write a briefing for someone who will not read it.

Open with the thesis in one sentence — the claim it exists to make.

Then the argument's load-bearing structure: each claim, the evidence carrying it, and where the chain breaks if one link fails. Include the specifics — numbers, dates, sample sizes, methods, names.

Separate the author's own findings from what they cite others for.

Name the strongest objection an informed reader would raise, and say whether the article answers it.

Plain declarative prose, every sentence load-bearing.

Done when every load-bearing claim appears with the evidence given for it.
